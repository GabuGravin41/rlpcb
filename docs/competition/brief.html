RL PCB

An AI design assistant for KiCad that finds parts, calculates values, places components and verifies wiring, and deliberately never draws the wires itself.
Dalton Omondi · Kenya · DTMES 2026 Africa Electronics Innovation Challenge — Project Competition (Built Stage) · September 2026

1. Problem

Designing a printed circuit board needs expertise that is scarce and slow to acquire. A beginner has to choose parts, derive component values from datasheets, connect hundreds of pins correctly and lay out a board that meets electrical rules. One wrong connection means a dead board, and every new fabrication run costs weeks and money that students and small hardware teams in Africa rarely have. Most have no senior engineer to review their work.

General AI chatbots don't fill this gap. They draw schematics badly, producing "ghost" wires that look connected but aren't, and they invent part numbers that don't exist. The help sounds confident but can't be trusted.

2. Solution and design

Core idea. The AI does everything around the drawing (research, parts, calculations, placement, instructions and verification), and the human draws the wires. The AI's design plan is a contract. The human's drawing is the source of truth. RL PCB checks one against the other, so every claim it makes can be verified.
1 · PlanRequirements → architecture → parts from the user's own KiCad libraries → values from shown formulas.
2 · ReviewElectrical rule check of the intent before drawing: power pins, floating inputs, decoupling, conflicts.
3 · PlaceSymbols grouped around the pins they serve, on hierarchical sheets; exact per-pin wiring instructions.
4 · Human wiresThe user draws the wires in KiCad. by design
5 · Verify → BoardDrawing vs plan (missing, shorted, near-miss wires); stackup, trace rules, placement, autorouting, DRC.

Reads .kicad_sch/.kicad_pcb directly (KiCad 4–10). Re-implements KiCad's connectivity rules: wires, junctions, labels, power symbols, hierarchical sheets, buses.
Design
Plan & review, symbol placement (no wires), wiring guide, verification, ghost-wire detector.

Stackup advisor, net classes, two-sided placement of passives, file & live moves, Freerouting autorouting, DRC, fabrication outputs.
Library index
Full-text search over every symbol and footprint installed (22,860 symbols and 15,450 footprints on the test laptop).
Knowledge
34 engineering guides and reference architectures, plus the user's own PDFs and datasheets.
Reference corpus
136 open-source designs (3,518 parts): "how do real designs wire this chip?"

The model reasons and explains; the tools do everything that must be exact (coordinates, netlists, geometry, calculations). Live control uses KiCad 9/10's official API; everything else works on the files, so KiCad 7/8 is covered too.

3. Innovation

  • A division of labour instead of generation. AI tools for electronics usually try to generate whole circuits and fail silently. RL PCB never draws wires and checks every result against an explicit plan.
  • Verification backed by KiCad. Its connectivity engine gives results identical to KiCad's own netlister (see §4), which is why its checks can be relied on.
  • Checks designed for weaker models. Every step reports a machine-checkable signal (errors, % connections verified, overlaps, unrouted count). The next version adds pre-verified circuit blocks so that smaller, cheaper models can use it offline. It all runs locally on a laptop with free tools (KiCad, Freerouting).

6. Impact and applications

  • Students, makers and early hardware startups designing controllers, sensors, power supplies, motor drivers, drones and industrial I/O. RL PCB acts as the reviewer they don't have, cutting failed board revisions.
  • Teaching. Every decision is explained with its formula or source, so it doubles as a tutor for university and technical-college electronics courses.
  • Local design capacity. It lowers the barrier to designing electronics in Africa, with fabrication abroad where needed, instead of importing finished designs.

7. Honest status and next steps

  • The drone board is a design generated and verified by the tool. It has not been fabricated or reviewed by an experienced engineer. Open items flagged for datasheet review: IMU reserved pins, TVS orientation, inductor saturation current ≥ 3 A, and USB back-feed into the battery rail.
  • About 10% of connections are left for manual routing, as are the critical paths (regulator hot loop, USB, IMU SPI). Remaining DRC items come from fine-pitch footprints under the default 0.2 mm clearance.
  • Built with extensive AI assistance (Claude) for implementation. I defined the problem, the no-wires architecture and the requirements, and tested it on my own KiCad setup.
